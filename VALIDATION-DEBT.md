# Validation debt

Things this library computes that are **not yet validated to the standard
the rest of it holds to**, each with what is missing and how it gets
discharged. This file exists because ISO/IEC 17025:2017 clause 7.11.2
NOTE 2 exempts commercial software "in general use" — which this is not —
so an accredited lab cannot adopt it without evidence, and the honest
version of that evidence includes what is still owed.

The rule everywhere here is `CLAUDE.md`'s: **a missing expected value
stays missing.** Nothing on this list is closed by inventing a number,
interpolating a neighbour, or writing this library's own output into a
reference table.

Discharged items move out of this file and into
`docs/validation-report.html`, which is generated by `stabilis validate`
and is the artifact that carries measured evidence.

---

## 1. Stable32 output parity — Greenhall EDOF and the estimators

**Status: open. Highest priority of the three.**

`stats/greenhall.py` reproduces the algorithm behind Stable32's `Alpha`,
`Min Sigma` and `Max Sigma` columns, and `stats/classical.py` reproduces
its sigma columns. What is validated today is the *published algorithm*:

- 18 627 parameter combinations agree with an independent implementation
  of the same paper to 1 part in 10⁹;
- eq. (11)'s term count reproduces every coefficient-array length this
  library builds, across five estimators and 250 averaging factors;
- the kernels match sequences worked out by hand from the paper's own
  generalized-autocovariance table, at exact integer equality;
- Monte-Carlo agreement with `2σ⁴/Var(σ̂²)` within 30 % for m ≥ 2.

What is **not** validated is agreement with Stable32's actual output. So
the claim this library may make today is *"implements the published
algorithm"*, not *"matches Stable32"* — and the theory page, the
validation report and the progress report all say exactly that.

**How it gets discharged.** One manual session. Stable32 1.62 runs
natively through WSL interop (`/mnt/c` is reachable; `wine` is not needed).
Run it over `PHASE.DAT`, the NBS suites and real WR/OCXO phase records,
export each statistic at all/octave/decade τ, and commit the literal
output to `tests/data/stable32/` with a `PROVENANCE.md` recording the
Stable32 version, the date, the input SHA-256 and the menu settings. Then
add an `interop` tier that asserts the `Min Sigma`/`Sigma`/`Max Sigma`
columns at the tolerance the exported significant figures support, and no
tighter.

**Do not** vendor `allantools` fixtures to shortcut this: LGPL-3.0 against
this repo's MPL-2.0. It is a test-only differential-testing dependency and
nothing is copied from it.

## 2. IEEE 1139 amplitude calibration of the synthesizer

**Status: open. Blocks `powerlaw.py`.**

`synth.power_law_noise` produces the right *slopes* — that is tested — but
carries **no calibrated `h_alpha`**. Its output has an arbitrary
amplitude. Consequences:

- the σ(τ) ↔ `h_alpha` conversion coefficients cannot be checked by
  generating noise of known `h_alpha` and recovering it;
- `powerlaw.py`'s design matrix therefore has no end-to-end test, only a
  structural one.

**How it gets discharged.** Calibrate the Kasdin–Walter output against the
closed forms that need no `f_h` and are already stated in
`theory/noise-types.md`: white FM `h₀/(2τ)`, flicker FM `2·ln2·h₋₁` (flat
in τ, a very sensitive joint test of synthesizer and estimator), and
random-walk FM `2π²h₋₂τ/3`. Then the ensemble mean of σ̂² over many
realizations pins the scale, and `h_alpha` becomes a settable parameter
rather than an uncalibrated constant.

## 3. Power-law conversion coefficients for white and flicker PM

**Status: open. Blocks `powerlaw.py`.**

The σ(τ) ↔ `S_y(f)` conversions for α = 0, −1, −2 are the three closed
forms above and are `f_h`-free. The two PM rows are not:

- both α = +2 and α = +1 depend on the measurement-system bandwidth `f_h`,
  which **is not identifiable from τ-domain data at all**. It is a
  required, recorded input; when defaulted it must set `assumed_fh=True`
  and propagate that flag into every report and plot legend, and rows
  violating `2π f_h τ ≥ 10` must be dropped;
- the modified-Allan coefficients for the same power laws are what break
  the white-PM / flicker-PM degeneracy, which is why the decomposition
  stacks AVAR and MVAR rows rather than fitting AVAR alone.

**How it gets discharged.** Transcribe from NIST SP 1065 (Riley,
*Handbook of Frequency Stability Analysis*) — **fetched and read, not
recalled** — recording the table and page in `references.md` the way the
Greenhall and Howe–Schlossberger rows already are. Then check the
transcription two ways that do not depend on it: against the three
`f_h`-free forms above where they overlap, and against item 2's calibrated
synthesizer end to end. Until both hold, the coefficients do not ship.

## 4. Instrument readers against real hardware exports

**Status: open, and not yet on the critical path.**

The 53100A / 53230A / 3120A readers (M4) will be written from format
documentation. A reader validated only against its own writer is validated
against nothing. Each binary reader needs a **paired export** — the same
measurement saved by the instrument in both its native format and ASCII —
before it may be trusted, and that pairing is the acceptance test.

## Not debt

For the avoidance of doubt, these are *already* validated and are not
waiting on anything:

- the classical estimators, against analytic identities at machine
  precision (linear frequency drift gives `avar = D²τ²/2` and `hvar = 0`
  exactly) and against an exact-rational oracle that `stats/` is forbidden
  to import;
- the χ² confidence-interval path, by measured Monte-Carlo containment,
  published in the validation report;
- η₁ and η₃, against `2σ⁴/Var(σ̂²)` measured directly;
- the incomplete gamma and inverse χ², against an exact `Decimal` oracle.
